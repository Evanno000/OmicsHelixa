07 | Scientific interpretation

Biological Interpretation

7.1 Protein sequence similarity

Protein sequence identity describes the proportion of aligned amino-acid positions that are identical between the predicted protein and its reference.

Query coverage describes the proportion of the predicted protein represented in the alignment. Reference coverage describes the proportion of the NCBI reference protein represented in the alignment.

7.2 ORF prediction

An open reading frame is a stretch of nucleotide sequence that can be translated from a start codon to an in-frame stop codon.

The OmicsBridge detector identifies candidate ORFs beginning with ATG and ending at an in-frame stop codon. These are computational predictions, not experimentally confirmed genes.

7.3 Six reading frames

Double-stranded DNA can be examined in six reading frames: three on the forward strand and three on the reverse-complement strand.

A reading frame determines how nucleotide triplets are grouped into codons. Different frames can produce different translated sequences from the same DNA region.

7.4 ORF overlap

Multiple ORFs may overlap because of alternative start positions, different reading frames or opposite-strand candidate regions.

Coordinate overlap alone does not establish independent transcription, translation or biological function.

7.5 Biological function

When a reference annotation identifies a protein such as the RuBisCO large subunit, its biological role can be investigated using the organism, gene annotation, protein sequence and supporting scientific literature.

RuBisCO catalyses carbon dioxide fixation in the Calvin-Benson cycle of photosynthesis.

Scientific limitations
  • Local pairwise alignment is not equivalent to an official NCBI BLAST search.
  • High sequence identity alone does not prove biological function.
  • ORF prediction does not demonstrate gene expression.
  • The current ORF detector uses ATG as its start codon and a minimum predicted length of 30 amino acids.
  • Overlap and containment are based on genomic coordinates and do not prove that candidates are separate functional genes.
